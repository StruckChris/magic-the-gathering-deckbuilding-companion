# Toolbox

## Recognize the Plan

The pool has selective access to a range of useful tools. Access might search, retrieve, or otherwise select from a constrained group of cards. A varied collection of answers without access is not automatically a toolbox.

## Shell Engine and Finish

The shell trades some efficiency for the ability to choose the relevant resource. Its engine repeatedly connects selection to useful deployment, potentially through creatures, artifacts, enchantments, or graveyard resources.

Finishers may be a chosen threat, a supported combination, or an accumulated board. The tutor or selector is infrastructure; identify what the found card actually accomplishes.

## Decisions and Failure Modes

Count access cost plus deployment cost and the timing window. A perfect answer that cannot be cast in time is not a usable answer. Verify restrictions on mana value, card type, colors, and zones.

Too many narrow tools can make ordinary draws awkward. Too few distinct tools can make selection redundant. Do not interpret access to a card as certainty that it will resolve or survive.

Some groups prefer less repetitive searching; ask about that preference when it materially affects the plan without automatically removing supplied tutors.

**Coaching question:** Which different game states do the accessible tools cover, and can the deck afford the full sequence?

Selective creature access connects situational tools and a combo package rather than relying solely on natural draws.

## Sustainable Advantage

A toolbox seeks the right function across different games, but searching is not itself net card advantage. In a supplied Commander pool, check access density, independently useful targets, replenishment, and total deployment cost. Established selectors may work under some spell limits, yet search restrictions or lost graveyard access can close particular routes. Include what the deck can do with naturally drawn tools.
