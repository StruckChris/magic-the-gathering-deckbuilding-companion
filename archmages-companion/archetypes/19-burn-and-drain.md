# Burn and Group Slug

## Recognize the Plan

The pool can repeatedly reduce life totals through damage, life loss, or punishment for common actions. Group slug usually describes broad, persistent pressure on multiple players rather than a sequence of combat attacks.

## Shell Engine and Finish

The shell makes time and ordinary resource use costly. Its engine might repeatedly trigger from supplied actions, recycle damage sources, or connect life-total pressure with replenishment. Some versions are aggressive; others use restrictions and survival tools to prolong a favorable race.

Finishers close the remaining life gap through burst output, accumulated triggers, or combat after resources have been strained. Distinguish damage from life loss and check which players each effect reaches.

## Decisions and Failure Modes

A one-opponent damage spell scales differently from an effect on each opponent. Symmetrical pressure may also put its controller within range of losing. Explain the actual survival plan instead of assuming incidental lifegain is sufficient.

Repeated punishment can attract attention before it produces a winning position. Avoid treating opponents' future spell casting as guaranteed fuel. What useful progress remains if they change their actions?

**Coaching question:** What lets you survive or control the race that your own effects are creating?

## Historical Reference

**Primary: Patrick Sullivan's Burn, Legacy, fifth at the SCG Legacy Open on March 27, 2011.** Selected package: 4 Lightning Bolt, 4 Chain Lightning, 4 Rift Bolt, 4 Fireblast, and 4 Grim Lavamancer. [Organizer-published list](https://old.starcitygames.com/decks/37491).

Redundant damage functions and repeatable sources connect ordinary draws to a closing burst. This is direct Burn evidence, not a multiplayer damage calculation.

**Related branch: Tiago Chan's Owling Mine, Standard, Pro Tour Honolulu 2006 Top 4.** Howling Mine, Ebony Owl Netsuke, and Exhaustion connect shared cards, constrained deployment, and punishment. [Pilot's report and list](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/almost-all-the-way-a-pt-honolulu-report-t4/). This illustrates resource punishment, not the historical origin of multiplayer politics.

## Sustainable Advantage

For Commander, account for three opposing life totals, available replenishment, and which payoffs affect each opponent. Across games, repeated triggers or usable damage sources can reduce reliance on one burst. Established triggers may function through casting restrictions, but opponents can change actions and deny expected fuel. Distinguish a self-sustained engine from one waiting for cooperation.

## Evolution and Overlap

Group slug borrows punishment from [prison](07-prison-and-stax.md); [aristocrats](08-aristocrats.md) supplies a different route to repeated life loss. Combat reach, spell-chain damage, and resource punishment share an ending but not identical engines. Choose the route the pool can actually sustain.
