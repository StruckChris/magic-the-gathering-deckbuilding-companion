# Prison and Aggro Stax

## Recognize the Plan

The pool restricts attacks, spell casting, untapping, resource use, or other actions. A tax asks for extra payment; a restriction limits what can happen. Exact wording determines which strategies it affects.

## Shell Engine and Finish

Prison controls available actions while building a way to win. Aggro-stax first develops pressure, then constrains the ability to catch up. The engine must keep the player's own deck progressing under the restrictions, perhaps through already-established permanents, token production, alternative resources, or permitted activations.

A finishing restriction secures a credible clock. It does not finish merely by making the game last longer.

A soft-lock engine maintains a difficult-to-attack or difficult-to-develop position while the deck keeps progressing. A pillow-fort package can serve that role without being a resource-denial deck. Explain the actions still available to opponents; neither an attack tax nor a collection of restrictions guarantees a complete lock.

## Decisions and Failure Modes

Identify why a nominally symmetrical effect hurts opponents more in the actual position. Do not assume that every opponent relies on the same resource. Do not combine restrictions without checking whether they also shut down the intended engine or recovery.

Ask about the group's expectations when this plan is relevant. Resource denial is neither automatically forbidden nor automatically welcome in casual play. Match the experience requested without moralizing about the archetype.

**Coaching question:** What can you continue doing after this restriction resolves, and how does that bring the game closer to an end?

## Historical Reference

**Primary: Roland Chang's Stax, Vintage, third at the SCG Power 9 Chicago event in July 2005.** Selected package: 4 Goblin Welder, 3 Crucible of Worlds, 4 Smokestack, 4 Tangle Wire, and 3 Sphere of Resistance. [Published list and contemporary testing analysis](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/the-many-flavors-of-t4ks-learning-cronstax-and-preparing-for-gencon/).

Recurring resources support restrictions that would otherwise consume both players' development. Stephen Menendian's discussion of different Stax builds shows the same denial shell using different supporting packages.

## Sustainable Advantage

For an owned Commander pool, map each restriction to the actions that remain available: established token production, permitted abilities, attacks, or another resource source. Across draws, look for several useful restriction-plus-development pairings, not merely more lock pieces. Account for upkeep, self-tax, and recovery. Graveyard-dependent replenishment is still vulnerable to graveyard restriction; an artifact-heavy engine is not independent of artifact denial.

## Evolution and Overlap

[Miracle Grow's tempo plan](03-tempo.md) illustrates pressure under mana denial; [Enchantress](15-enchantress.md) illustrates replenishment behind a defensive package. These are distinct relatives, not interchangeable lists. Aggro-stax develops a clock underneath restrictions; a pillow-fort engine may instead protect a slower accumulation. In either case, identify how maintaining the position advances the finish.
