# Draw Go Permission

## Recognize the Plan

The deck often draws, evaluates, and passes while retaining useful instant-speed choices. Having blue cards or several counterspells does not by itself make a deck draw-go.

Draw-Go is a style of control that seeks to stymie important opposing plays through sustained countermagic pressure while accumulating its own resources. It is about preserving choices and selecting exchanges, not literally countering every spell or refusing all main-phase plays.

## Shell Engine and Finish

The shell preserves flexibility and answers the exchanges that would undermine its plan. The engine must turn that purchased time into progress: repeated card access, reusable interaction, flash threats, or complementary permanents that establish a protected attacking position.

Finishers need to be deployable without abandoning all the control that made the position possible. They may also be parts of the engine rather than unrelated late-game cards.

## Decisions and Failure Modes

Held mana has value only if the player has relevant ways to use it. Identify a productive alternative when no answer is required, while respecting the actual timing permissions. An ordinary sorcery-speed permanent cannot automatically be cast during an opponent's end step.

In multiplayer, selective permission matters: spending a card on every opposing action is rarely a sustainable plan. Do not assume the rest of the table will always cooperate in answering threats.

**Coaching question:** If nothing needs countering this turn cycle, what can you accomplish with the resources you held open?

## Historical Reference

**Primary: Randy Buehler's historical Standard Draw-Go, reproduced in Wizards' 2014 teaching article.** Selected package: 4 Counterspell, 4 Whispers of the Muse, 4 Nevinyrral's Disk, and 1 Rainbow Efreet. Permission and resets buy time; repeatable card access sustains choices; a small threat package finishes. [Published Draw-Go list](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/mana-curve-2014-03-24).

**Evolution reference: Philipp Schonegger's CounterTop Miracles, Legacy, fourth at Grand Prix New Jersey 2014.** Its selected package includes 4 Counterbalance, 4 Sensei's Divining Top, 4 Terminus, 2 Entreat the Angels, and 3 Jace, the Mind Sculptor. [Pilot's report and full list](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/one-journey-one-miracle-a-grand-prix-new-jersey-story/).

CounterTop Miracles is a recognizable functional evolution of permission: make opposing spell deployment conditional while building one's own resources. Counterbalance and Top supply the control shell; selection and board management sustain the position, while Angels or Jace can close it. Its permanent-based permission differs from classic Draw-Go's held instant-speed answers, but the enduring resource-and-choice lesson connects them.

## Sustainable Advantage

Across games, several relevant answers and productive alternatives to answering make passing useful in different positions. In Commander, identify supplied instant-speed draw, activations, or flash threats so unused response mana can become progress. Established triggered permission can conserve resources, but setup, top manipulation, and protection still have costs. Trading one card for every opposing spell across three players is not self-sustaining.

## Evolution and Overlap

[Tap-out control](06-tap-out-control.md) can use similar answers but commits resources differently. [Prison](07-prison-and-stax.md) also constrains choices; [tempo](03-tempo.md) uses those windows to exploit an earlier clock. These are overlapping methods, not claims that every permission list shares one exact ancestor. Explain the player's actual engine and finisher after identifying the shell.
