# Reanimator

## Recognize the Plan

The pool can place worthwhile creatures in a graveyard and return them to useful play. Having large creatures without setup and return effects is not a functioning reanimator plan.

## Shell Engine and Finish

The shell changes how threats reach the battlefield. Its engine links setup, retrieval, and ongoing value, potentially turning discard or self-mill into useful preparation. Some versions emphasize a large early threat; others build a slower cycle of sacrifice, return, and repeated effects.

Finishers are the returned threats or interactions that actually close the game. A large mana value alone does not make a suitable target. Check immediate effect, survival, attack timing, and the ability to compete with the expected board.

## Decisions and Failure Modes

Returning a creature directly is not casting it. A cast trigger cannot be credited automatically. A one-shot reanimation effect may have a delay, sacrifice condition, or other limitation.

Assess graveyard exposure without assuming every opposing deck always has graveyard hate. Consider supplied alternative deployment or recovery options. A collection with several expensive targets and one return spell may need a different main plan.

**Coaching question:** Which useful turn sequence connects your setup card, return effect, and target, and what remains if one stage is interrupted?

## Historical Reference

**Primary: Brian Selden's RecSur, Standard, 1998 World Championship winner, reproduced in a 2014 retrospective.** Selected package: 4 Survival of the Fittest, 4 Recurring Nightmare, 4 Wall of Blossoms, and 1 Verdant Force. [Published list](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/more-expensive-casting-costs-2014-03-31).

This earlier root links selective creature access, graveyard setup, and repeated deployment. The engine can reuse useful entry effects as well as bring an expensive body into play without its normal casting cost.

## Sustainable Advantage

Look for supplied setup that remains useful before a return spell appears, multiple viable targets, and replenishment after a first attempt. Across games, those connections reduce dependence on one ideal discard-and-reanimate hand. Alternative deployment can bypass a threat's casting cost, but not the cost of the enabler, timing restrictions, or graveyard access. Name an available fallback when the graveyard is unavailable rather than claiming recursion automatically provides resilience.

## Evolution and Overlap

Reanimator overlaps with [toolbox](17-toolbox.md) when it selects appropriate creatures, [aristocrats](08-aristocrats.md) when sacrifices supply recurring material, and [blink](13-blink-and-recursion.md) when entry effects carry the engine. Early large-threat deployment and slower repeated-value plans share a route onto the battlefield but need different support and finishing windows.
