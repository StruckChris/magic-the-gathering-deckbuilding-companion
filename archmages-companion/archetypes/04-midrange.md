# Midrange

## Recognize the Plan

The pool offers meaningful threats and flexible answers across several stages of the game. Midrange is not simply a pile of individually celebrated cards or spells with medium mana costs.

## Shell Engine and Finish

The shell aims to make productive exchanges while developing threats that remain relevant. It can defend against a faster opponent and pressure a slower one. Its engine might combine useful creatures with recursion, repeated combat rewards, or permanents that provide both board presence and card access.

Finishers convert accumulated material into a decisive attack or another supplied ending. Do not assume winning several exchanges creates inevitability against every other engine.

## Decisions and Failure Modes

Flexibility has a cost if every draw performs a different job and no package reinforces another. Identify the common resource conversion. Also test whether a hand full of expensive value cards can survive long enough to use them.

A universal answer is not always the right play when developing a threat would better constrain an opponent. The player's role depends on the actual game, not just a permanent archetype label.

**Coaching question:** In this position, who benefits if everyone gets three more turns, and what should your deck do about it?

## Historical Reference

**Primary: Owen Turtenwald's historical Standard Jund, reproduced in Wizards' 2017 midrange guide.** No particular event finish is assigned here. Selected package: 4 Farseek, 4 Huntmaster of the Fells, 4 Thragtusk, 3 Liliana of the Veil, and 2 Rakdos's Return. [Published list and construction explanation](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/how-build-midrange-2017-02-07).

The lesson is role flexibility: development, resource-producing threats, and answers can support defense or pressure depending on the opponent.

## Sustainable Advantage

Across games, cards with useful floors reduce the number of draws waiting for a special partner. For Commander, connect those cards through available replenishment, recurring effects, or combat rewards so each exchange leaves a next play. Existing permanents may preserve value when casting slows, but expensive follow-ups still need resources. A collection of independent threats is not automatically an engine that can outlast several opponents.

## Evolution and Overlap

[Goodstuff](22-goodstuff.md) describes flexible card selection; midrange describes how the deck changes roles. [Zoo](21-zoo.md) is pressure-first rather than an older name for all midrange. Changes in expected opponents can move the engine toward recursion, proactive control, or faster attacks without making every variant a wholly new shell.
