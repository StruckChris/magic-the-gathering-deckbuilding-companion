# Ramp and Big Mana

## Recognize the Plan

The pool can increase mana access ahead of ordinary land development and has useful ways to spend it. Ramp creates resources; it does not by itself determine how the deck wins.

## Shell Engine and Finish

The shell invests early resources to make larger or multiple actions available sooner. The engine might pair land development with draw, reuse scalable activations, or deploy threats that provide further resources.

Finishers can be substantial creatures, scalable spells, repeated activations, or other owned payoffs. The actual colors and restrictions on the produced mana matter.

## Decisions and Failure Modes

Compare the time spent accelerating with the pressure already facing the player. A hand of ramp without access to payoffs can stall; a hand of expensive payoffs without acceleration can do nothing. Selection and draw help connect the halves only when they are affordable in the sequence.

Creature mana, artifact mana, and additional lands expose the deck to different disruption. None is a universal default. A cost reduction does not necessarily generate mana or pay activation costs.

**Coaching question:** If the deck reaches its larger mana total, which supplied actions will use that mana productively even without the best payoff?

## Historical Reference

**Primary: Terry Soh's Troll and Nail, Standard, his 2005 Invitational deck.** His firsthand report supplies the list: the Urza land trio, 4 Sakura-Tribe Elder, 4 Kodama's Reach, 4 Sylvan Scrying, and 3 Tooth and Nail are selected components. [Pilot's report and list](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/troll-and-nail/).

Acceleration and land access support deploying substantial creature effects. Soh's [follow-up version and testing discussion](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/troll-and-nail-v-2/) provide a concrete example of adapting an engine and supporting cards after more games, rather than treating the first list as permanent.

## Sustainable Advantage

Ramp is an input; selection, replenishment, and useful spending options connect that input to repeatable progress. In Commander, check whether ordinary draws contain actions at several resource levels, not only the largest payoff. Lands, creatures, and artifacts have different vulnerabilities. Diversifying available sources can reduce dependence on one category, but should serve actual needs rather than mandate a generic rock package.

## Evolution and Overlap

[Lands](16-lands.md) can turn development itself into card flow, while [toolbox](17-toolbox.md) can make expensive deployment selective. Ramp can support aggro, control, or combo. Preserve the player's intended payoff and adjust the supporting engine when repeated games reveal unused mana or stranded expensive cards.
