# Typal Synergy

## Recognize the Plan

Typal, also commonly called tribal, organizes a deck around creature types. Shared type can enable a plan but is not itself a complete explanation of that plan.

## Shell Engine and Finish

First identify what the type package supports: aggressive attacks, mana production, graveyard reuse, spell interaction, token growth, or another role. The engine is the particular set of type rewards and supplied creatures that turns those actions into progress.

Finishers might be group scaling, evasive combat, a type-supported combo, or a threat that exploits the accumulated resources. Two decks sharing a type can have different shells and engines.

## Decisions and Failure Modes

Balance type density with the cards that actually make the deck function. A thematic creature that performs a needed role may fit; another that shares only the type may not. An off-type support card is not automatically a thematic failure if the player is comfortable with it.

Verify which objects receive the type or reward, including zones and tokens. Do not assume a type-changing effect applies everywhere or that every creature in a themed product has the relevant type.

**Coaching question:** What do these creatures let you accomplish together that the same number of unrelated creatures would not?

## Historical Reference

**Primary: Luis Scott-Vargas's Elves, Extended, winner of Pro Tour Berlin 2008.** Selected package: 4 Heritage Druid, 4 Nettle Sentinel, 4 Glimpse of Nature, 4 Wirewood Symbiote, and 1 Grapeshot. [Pilot's tournament report and list](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/feature-article-pro-tour-berlin-tournament-report-part-1-winner/).

Small creatures provide mana and material for a replenished spell chain; the finishing spell converts that chain into damage. Shared type enables the mechanism rather than substituting for one.

## Sustainable Advantage

Across games, type members that perform necessary jobs can connect different hands to the same plan. For Commander, find the owned mana, draw, reuse, and attack rewards rather than assuming an entire historical package exists. Creature-based resources can reduce dependence on rocks, but creature removal, summoning restrictions, and spell limits can still interrupt the engine. Count several payoffs sharing one enabler as a common dependency.

## Evolution and Overlap

Typal decks can be [aggro](01-creature-aggro.md), [ramp](10-ramp.md), [tempo](03-tempo.md), or [combo](12-combo.md). Changing between combat amplification and resource conversion changes the engine even when most creature types stay the same. A successful typal reference teaches coordinated roles, not a mandate that every themed deck use its ending.
