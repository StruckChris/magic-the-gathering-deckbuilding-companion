# Zoo

## Recognize the Plan

The pool supports efficient early attackers across colors, with removal to clear combat and sometimes direct damage to finish a weakened opponent. Zoo is an aggressive archetype family, not a synonym for midrange or a requirement to use a particular creature type.

## Shell Engine and Finish

The shell spends early mana on an efficient clock and keeps opponents from stabilizing. Its engine can be complementary plays rather than a fixed loop: useful bodies keep attacking while interaction preserves their damage. In Commander, supplied combat rewards, replenishment, or scaling effects can help that pressure survive longer games.

Finishers may be a decisive combat enhancement, evasive pressure, or damage that reaches an opponent combat can no longer finish. Use only what the pool supports. The absence of an infinite loop does not mean the deck has no engine.

## Decisions and Failure Modes

An efficient creature in a 20-life duel is not automatically a meaningful threat against three 40-life opponents. Explain how the supplied deck scales, replaces losses, or concentrates pressure. More colors also ask more of the mana base; do not prescribe expensive fixing to preserve a historical color combination.

Some Zoo variants play a larger game, but the label does not make all flexible creature decks Zoo. Distinguish its pressure-first approach from midrange's greater emphasis on changing roles.

**Coaching question:** What lets these early attackers continue mattering after the table develops larger boards?

## Historical Reference

**Primary: Craig Jones's Zoo, Standard, runner-up at Pro Tour Honolulu 2006.** Selected package: 4 Kird Ape, 4 Savannah Lions, 4 Watchwolf, 4 Lightning Helix, and 4 Char. [Pilot's report and list](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/the-16000-lightning-helix-a-pt-honolulu-report-part-1-2nd/).

Efficient attackers establish a clock; removal and burn help it continue or finish when combat becomes difficult. This is an aggressive reference, not another name for every midrange deck.

## Sustainable Advantage

Across games, redundant early attackers and flexible reach make different opening hands serve the same pressure plan. In Commander, identify owned scaling, combat rewards, or replenishment that keep those bodies relevant against larger life totals and boards. Established attacks can continue through some casting restrictions, but stalled combat still needs a solution. An efficient curve does not justify spending every resource when holding a useful response is better.

## Evolution and Overlap

[Creature aggro](01-creature-aggro.md) is the broader family; [midrange](04-midrange.md) more readily changes between pressure and defense. Zoo variants can borrow larger threats or recurring value when expected opponents demand it. The shared principle is coordinated pressure, not fixed colors, expensive fixing, or creature-type identity.
