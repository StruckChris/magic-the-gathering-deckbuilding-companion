# Archetypical Shell Library

Read after the [Aggro / Combo / Control primer](../primers/01-aggro-combo-control.md). These twenty-two entries provide broad starting coverage for public users, not a catalogue of one person's decks or an exhaustive taxonomy of Magic.

Recognize a shell from what the supplied cards can do. Show the evidence and explain alternatives. An archetype is a useful starting hypothesis, not an instruction to acquire a stock list.

## How to Use an Entry

Explain its operating approach, then identify which parts of the supplied pool can form its engine and finish. Do not read a recognition column as sufficient evidence by itself. One sacrifice outlet does not establish aristocrats; a few counterspells do not establish draw-go.

The entries intentionally overlap. Some names describe an operating style; others name well-recognized synergy families that can organize several styles. For example, artifacts or a creature type can support aggro, combo, or control. Their primers make that second decision explicit rather than treating material type as a complete plan.

Counters, lifegain, Equipment, theft, milling, graveyard casting, and other themes can be engines or finishing routes inside these shells. Expand the library when a theme needs its own operating explanation; do not force every mechanic into a separate archetype.

## Reference Organization

Each numbered primer is the canonical home of its historical explanation and direct external links. Read its sections in order: recognize the plan; shell, engine, and finish; decisions; historical reference; sustainable advantage; evolution and overlap. Its primary list is distinct from the other primers' primary lists. Shared secondary examples explain genuine overlap rather than suggesting the families are mutually exclusive.

The [competitive list library](../references/competitive-lists.md#archetype-coverage) is a navigation and provenance index. It does not maintain a second copy of the explanations. Competitive lists teach construction methods; the political primer uses documented Commander evidence instead of inventing tournament ancestry. The [reference guide](../guides/rules-and-competitive-context.md) explains verification.

The 2000s-era foundation concerns coherent advantage across many games, not copying one tournament's exact cards. Four-of redundancy, flexible roles, resource management, and revisions for changing opponents provide lessons to translate into an owned singleton pool. Later teaching sources and earlier roots help explain the method's continuity.

When expanding a primer, update its explanation and source locally, then update the index entry. Keep every project filename unique so retrieval or upload systems do not confuse separate files sharing a name.
