# Blink and Recursion Value

## Recognize the Plan

The pool has useful effects on entering or leaving the battlefield and ways to revisit those effects. Blink generally moves a permanent out and back; recursion retrieves resources from a graveyard. They can support related plans but are not mechanically interchangeable.

## Shell Engine and Finish

Choose an operating style: midrange development, repeated control, or a specific combo. The engine repeatedly uses supplied draw, removal, token, or resource effects through the available blink or return tools.

Finishers might be accumulated bodies, an interaction that establishes a decisive position, or a verified loop. Reusing a small effect indefinitely across ordinary turns does not necessarily create a fast enough clock for the intended table.

## Decisions and Failure Modes

Count the full price of each cycle and whether the effect returns immediately or later. A target may be restricted by controller, type, or other wording. Do not assume tokens or attached enhancements behave like ordinary reusable creatures.

The best entry effect in isolation may cost too much to repeat while developing. Compare smaller effects that fit available mana. Also test the deck with the repeat enabler removed, not only with every value creature active.

**Coaching question:** Which repeated effect advances the actual game plan, rather than just generating activity?

## Sustainable Advantage

Across draws, several useful targets and more than one means of reuse can keep the engine from depending on a single pairing. Prefer supplied effects that accomplish needed jobs even before repetition is available. Some established blink abilities can operate under casting limits, but their costs and timing still apply. Graveyard recursion and battlefield blink expose different resources; do not treat either as protection against every kind of removal.

## Evolution and Overlap

This engine can power [tap-out control](06-tap-out-control.md), [midrange](04-midrange.md), or [combo](12-combo.md). As the surrounding game changes, the selected targets can change while the reuse method remains. Choosing between repeated draw, removal, and board production is engine design, not simply maximizing the number of entry triggers.
