# Opponent Card Reference Guide

These four built-in references help the Companion answer questions such as "What might my opponent do with this?", "Is that banned?", and "What should I watch for next?" They are recognition material, not deckbuilding prescriptions, shopping lists, or a reason to assume competitive intentions.

## Choose the Right List

| Reference | What it records | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Game Changers](commander-game-changers.md) | The dated official designation and all listed card names | A ban, an automatic threat priority, or a deck's entire power level |
| [Banned cards](commander-banned-cards.md) | Named Commander bans, role-specific restrictions, and category exclusions | Other formats' legality or the group's agreed house rules |
| [Common staples and goodstuff](common-staples-reference.md) | Recognizable interaction, resource, land, and finishing roles, with contextual alternatives | An official category, measured popularity ranking, or compulsory inclusions |
| [Beware and watch list](opponent-watch-list.md) | Less-obvious enablers and high-impact interactions outside the stored Game Changers list | A second ban list, proof of malicious intent, or automatic "must remove" instructions |

The categories can overlap conceptually: a staple may be a Game Changer or a contextual watch card. Consult official status separately. The common-card and watch lists are curated starting coverage, not exhaustive databases.

The watch list spans all five colors, multicolor, and colorless cards and lands. Preserve varied strategies when revising it: combat, tokens, counters, spellslinger, enchantress, graveyards, resources, and restrictions, not just artifact combo-control. Do not build it from a particular player's private deck packages. "Outside the Game Changers list" describes official membership, not an official near-Game-Changer power tier.

## Answer the Board Question

1. Identify the exact card, public board state, relevant mana, and what the user wants to know.
2. Explain its immediate function in plain language.
3. Describe likely follow-ups conditionally: "with reusable creatures, this can..." rather than asserting unseen cards are in hand.
4. Distinguish a present winning line, an engine accumulating advantage, and a merely possible future combination.
5. Explain the meaningful response window and cost of waiting, using verified rules.
6. Consider the whole table and the user's available interaction. A recognized strong card is not always the most urgent threat.

Answer with a useful short form: **What it does -> What makes it dangerous here -> What to watch next -> When interaction can matter.** A focused question does not need all four lists recited.

## Timing and Information

An opponent's activated ability may remain on the stack after its source is removed. Paying a cost is not a separate response window. Mana abilities usually do not use the stack, and priority does not pass during a resolving spell. Verify the actual ability before suggesting when to act. [Current rules](https://magic.wizards.com/en/rules).

Different tutors reveal different information. Use only what was actually revealed or is otherwise public; do not treat a searched card as known by default. Recognizing a possible combo does not prove that its missing pieces are present. If the user asks for exact play, trace the sequence and remaining resources rather than relying on a card's reputation.

## Opponent Context Is Not a Purchase Request

The bot can explain cards outside the user's collection when they are an opponent's cards or requested reference examples. That does not authorize adding them to the user's deck. When advising on responses, use the supplied deck or available pool; otherwise discuss response functions without inventing owned answers.

When the question turns to building, compare owned alternatives by function and fit. The [goodstuff comparison guidance](common-staples-reference.md#recognizing-alternatives) uses Brainstone to illustrate how color access, timing, and artifact support can change an evaluation. Recognizing a staple should help explain a role, not exclude a less familiar card from consideration.

"Beware" means understand the interaction, not avoid the card, condemn the player, or build around a presumed specific matchup. Maintain the player's own plan while explaining how to adapt decisions.

## Keeping References Current

The official lists carry a checked date and sources. Recheck them for current-status questions, an announced update, or a conflicting report; do not claim a permanent snapshot is live data. Replace superseded entries in place rather than creating competing daily lists. Reconcile the watch list when an official designation changes.

The staple and watch explanations are coaching judgments. Recheck exact card text for detailed rules or a specific combo using the [lookup guide](../guides/card-lookup-and-access.md). EDHREC access restrictions and Scryfall API policies still apply. Never claim verification that the available tools could not perform.
