# Opponent Watch List

**Purpose:** Recognize enabling cards across Commander whose importance may not be obvious from their size, price, reputation, or official designation. This built-in starter reference contains 42 cards: six examples in each of seven sections. Prepared 1 October 2026.

This is a curated teaching list, not an official "almost Game Changers" tier or a ranking of the next-most-powerful cards. None of these names appears in the stored [Game Changers](commander-game-changers.md) or [all-role Commander bans](commander-banned-cards.md) checked on that date. Recheck official status when it matters.

The sections cover white, blue, black, red, green, multicolor, and colorless cards and lands. Colored artifacts are filed under their colors; lands are grouped together regardless of color identity. This layout ensures broad introductory coverage, not equal power or equal prevalence across colors.

Use the [opponent reference guide](opponent-card-reference-guide.md). Explain the visible mechanism and what additional conditions make it important. An engine gaining resources, a restriction, a combat payoff, and an assembled winning line are different concerns. Do not declare a hidden combo assembled or recommend removing everything on sight.

Select public examples for their teaching value across strategies. Do not mine the author's private deck conversations for a catalogue of his engines or combo packages. Familiar cards can appear on their own merits; private deck-specific sequences are not public reference material. Detailed combo walkthroughs belong in a requested, independently verified explanation, not in every recognition entry.

## White

| Card | What it enables | What to watch or clarify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anointed Procession | Multiplies tokens its controller would create. | It amplifies creature and noncreature tokens but supplies none on its own. Look at existing producers and payoffs. |
| Cathars' Crusade | Creature entries put counters across its controller's creature board. | Several entries can turn small bodies into a serious clock. Count entry events, not just spells cast. |
| Grand Abolisher | Restricts opposing spells and certain activated abilities during its controller's turn. | It can make a deployment or finishing turn harder to contest. It does not shut off every ability or protect the controller on other turns. |
| Rest in Peace | Exiles graveyards on entry and replaces cards or tokens going to graveyards with exile. | Recursion and death-trigger plans can both be affected. Removing it later does not return already-exiled cards. |
| Aven Mindcensor | Restricts opponents' library searches to the top four cards. | Flash makes an announced search a relevant window. It restricts searching, not ordinary drawing or looking at cards. |
| Ethersworn Canonist | Restricts further nonartifact spells after a player has cast one that turn. | Artifact-heavy development may continue. This is not a blanket one-spell-per-turn rule. |

Grand Abolisher's ability limits are covered in the [Commander Masters release notes](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/commander-masters-release-notes). Its presence does not guarantee an uninterrupted win.

## Blue

| Card | What it enables | What to watch or clarify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Merchant Scroll | A two-mana sorcery finding a revealed blue instant and putting it into hand. | The actual revealed card and remaining mana indicate its role. It is neither an instant itself nor a tutor for any blue card. |
| Intruder Alarm | Creature entries untap all creatures, while normal creature untaps are restricted. | Repeatable entries and productive tap abilities can become an engine. The effect is symmetrical and still needs inputs. |
| Kindred Discovery | A chosen creature type's entries and attacks generate cards. | Token production or a wide typal board can make the draw substantial. It does not require combat damage to connect. |
| Mystic Remora | Opponents' noncreature spells can provide cards unless they make its payment. | Spell volume, cumulative upkeep, and available mana determine how much advantage it gains. |
| Training Grounds | Reduces activation costs of creatures its controller controls. | Inspect the supported creature abilities and remaining colored costs. It does not reduce every permanent's abilities or make all activations free. |
| Hullbreaker Horror | Subsequent spell casts can return opposing spells or nonland permanents to hand. | Cheap spells can become repeated interaction. The controller still needs castable spells and resources; returning a spell is not countering it. |

Recognize "Merchant's Scroll" as a likely reference to **Merchant Scroll** when context is clear. Hullbreaker's two return modes are documented in the [Innistrad Remastered release notes](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/innistrad-remastered-release-notes).

## Black

| Card | What it enables | What to watch or clarify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pitiless Plunderer | Other creatures its controller controls dying produce Treasures. | A sacrifice-and-replenishment engine may refund its costs. It is not a sacrifice outlet itself. |
| Blood Artist | Creature deaths cause targeted life loss and life gain. | It sees opponents' creatures die too, so a board wipe can produce a threatening number of triggers. |
| Grave Pact | Its controller's creature deaths force other players to sacrifice creatures. | Disposable creatures and repeated sacrifice can restrict opposing boards without targeting them. The sacrificed creatures are chosen by their controllers. |
| Necrotic Ooze | Gains activated abilities of creature cards in all graveyards. | Read the actual graveyards. It does not acquire every static or triggered ability and is not a complete combo by itself. |
| Razaketh, the Foulblooded | Life and other creatures can be converted into repeated tutors. | Spare bodies and sufficient life can make successive searches possible. The search does not cast or deploy the found card. |
| Living Death | Exchanges existing creature boards for creature cards already in graveyards through a specific exile, sacrifice, and return sequence. | Compare every graveyard before treating it as a simple wipe. Entry and death payoffs can matter as much as the returning bodies. |

[Blood Artist's simultaneous-death ruling](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/double-masters-2022-release-notes-2022-06-24) and [Grave Pact's individual sacrifice triggers](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/commander-masters-release-notes) illustrate why removal can itself feed an engine.

## Red

| Card | What it enables | What to watch or clarify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Impact Tremors | Its controller's creature entries damage each opponent. | Repeated or mass token creation becomes pressure without attacking. Summoning sickness does not stop these triggers. |
| Goblin Bombardment | Creatures can be sacrificed for damage without a mana or tap cost. | Count available bodies and replenishment. Sacrifice is paid before another player can respond to the damage ability. |
| Storm-Kiln Artist | Casting or copying instant and sorcery spells produces Treasures. | Spell chains can generate their next payment. Separate spells cast from copies when checking other payoffs. |
| Aggravated Assault | A paid sorcery-timing activation untaps its controller's creatures and adds combat and main phases. | Additional attacks are finite unless the required mana can keep being replenished. Untapping alone does not supply it. |
| Repercussion | Damage dealt to a creature also produces damage to that creature's controller. | Combat or mass damage can become player damage. It is symmetrical and can threaten its own controller too. |
| Blood Moon | Changes nonbasic lands into Mountains. | Colors and utility abilities may disappear. Check actual basic lands and alternative mana rather than assuming every deck is locked out. |

Storm-Kiln Artist's cast-and-copy trigger is explained in the [Commander Masters release notes](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/commander-masters-release-notes). It illustrates resource generation within spellslinger play, not an automatic infinite sequence.

## Green

| Card | What it enables | What to watch or clarify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Earthcraft | Untapped creatures can be used to untap basic lands. | Bodies and productive basic lands can sustain further actions. The basic-land restriction matters, and the card does not replenish creatures by itself. |
| The Great Henge | A large creature can discount a source of mana, life, counters, and continuing creature-entry draw. | Its draw requires nontoken creatures. Distinguish accelerating the next play from an already-complete loop. |
| Finale of Devastation | Finds a creature from the library or graveyard and, at sufficiently large X, adds a team boost and haste. | The announced X distinguishes ordinary assembly from a potential combat finish. |
| Allosaurus Shepherd | Protects its controller's green spells from countering and can turn Elves into larger attackers. | Countering spells and removing a resolved permanent remain different questions. Its combat activation still needs mana. |
| Hardened Scales | Increases batches of +1/+1 counters placed on its controller's creatures. | Repeated small counter events can scale differently from one large event. It adds a counter rather than doubling the batch. |
| Scute Swarm | Land entries produce creatures, switching to copies of itself once enough lands are controlled. | Multiple land entries can multiply future triggers. Count actual land access and the six-land threshold. |

[The Great Henge and Finale](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/commander-masters-release-notes) demonstrate different roles: sustained development versus assembly that can become a finisher. [Hardened Scales' replacement effect](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/double-masters-2022-release-notes-2022-06-24) is additive, not a generic doubler.

## Multicolor

| Card | What it enables | What to watch or clarify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mayhem Devil | Any player's sacrificed permanents generate targeted damage. | Treasures, fetch lands, and sacrifice outlets can all matter. It does not itself grant permission to sacrifice anything. |
| Shalai and Hallar | +1/+1 counters placed on its controller's creatures become damage to an opponent. | Counter engines may threaten life totals without combat. Identify the actual source and frequency of counters. |
| Sythis, Harvest's Hand | Enchantment spells generate a card and life when cast. | Cheap enchantment density can replenish deployment. An enchantment entering without being cast is a different event. |
| Simic Ascendancy | Creature counters accumulate growth counters toward an upkeep win condition. | Check the counter threshold and upkeep timing; reaching the number is not an immediate win. |
| Muldrotha, the Gravetide | Reuses a land and one permanent spell of each type from its controller's graveyard during each of its turns. | Costs and normal timing still apply. Graveyard access gives repeated options, not unlimited free casting. |
| Yuriko, the Tiger's Shadow | Ninja combat damage supplies cards and causes table-wide life loss based on revealed mana value. | Small attackers may threaten much more than their printed power suggests. Access through commander ninjutsu is not a spell cast. |

[Yuriko's original release notes](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/commander-2018-edition-release-notes-2018-07-27) distinguish commander ninjutsu from casting. Multicolor examples cover sacrifice, counters, enchantress, recursion, alternate wins, and combat rather than one color pair's combo package.

## Colorless and Lands

| Card | What it enables | What to watch or clarify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ashnod's Altar | Sacrificed creatures become two colorless mana each without tapping the Altar. | Finite creatures mean finite mana unless they can be replenished profitably. Death payoffs can also matter. |
| Skullclamp | Equipped creatures dying yield two cards each. | Its toughness reduction can kill small tokens; anthems, equip costs, and available bodies affect that exchange. |
| Panharmonicon | Adds triggers when an artifact or creature entering triggers qualifying abilities of its controller's permanents. | It does not double all triggers or replacement effects. Identify the actual entry payoff. |
| Thornbite Staff | Gives the equipped creature an untap trigger when a creature dies. | A useful tap ability and repeated deaths can support reuse. The equipment alone supplies neither the full input nor a winning outlet. |
| Krark-Clan Ironworks | Sacrificed artifacts become colorless mana. | Disposable artifacts, recursion, and death or graveyard payoffs may sustain development. Count the cost to replace what was sacrificed. |
| Cabal Coffers | Pays an activation cost to generate black mana according to Swamps controlled. | Count actual Swamps and land-type changes. This land has a black color identity despite being filed with lands here. |

Ashnod's Altar and Krark-Clan Ironworks have mana abilities that do not use the stack. Sacrifices paid as costs cannot be undone by responding afterward. Other parts of a proposed sequence may provide interaction windows; verify those using the [current rules](https://magic.wizards.com/en/rules).

## Verification and Scope

These are short recognition notes, not full Oracle text, a certification of every interaction, or a comprehensive list of important cards. Sources support selected mechanics; they do not establish popularity, a universal priority order, or that the surrounding strategy always works.

Verify exact text and any requested sequence through the [lookup and access guide](../guides/card-lookup-and-access.md). Count required pieces and resources with the [combo primer](../primers/05-combo-structure.md). Explain two-, three-, and four-card dependencies when relevant without assuming every engine aims at infinity.

For an opponent question, explain the named card and conditional follow-ups. For "what can I do?", use known available interaction or describe the missing function. This reference does not authorize purchases, assume ownership, or replace the player's own plan with a matchup-specific deck.
