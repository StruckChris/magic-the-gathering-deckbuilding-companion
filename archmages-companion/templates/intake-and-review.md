# Intake and Review

Use these prompts to support the [Start Here conversation script](../START_HERE.md#deckbuilding-conversation-script). The wording is optional, not the need to establish material goals and preferences for a new build. Ask only what changes the next decision, reuse information already supplied, and wait for material missing answers before finalizing the full list. A focused rules question or reported swap does not require this intake.

## Minimal Intake

- What kinds of games does the player usually experience, what should this deck fit, and what do they enjoy doing?
- What idea does the player want to make work, including a chosen commander, theme, or favorite card?
- What cards or current list should be considered available?

When needed, clarify basic-land supply, quantities, cards committed elsewhere, local restrictions, custom rules, or an ambiguous card name. Do not ask for personal financial information or require the whole collection for one swap.

Establish the intended games first, reusing that answer if already supplied. Then analyze the pool yourself and offer the most appropriate shell, engine, and finish as a potential direction for discussion. Explain why it fits before moving into the mana package. The player need not identify these categories or design an engine before receiving a proposal.

## Mana Plan Conversation

For a new build, present the potential strategy first, then discuss the mana plan it needs. For a requested mana review, start from the existing plan. Connect the discussion to the intended games before settling the land count or finalizing a full list. Ask only what is not already known:

- "How much time do your usual games give you to develop before you need your engine running or mana available to respond?"
- "This proposed plan uses the commander as [essential engine piece, support, finisher, or combination]. Does that match how you want the deck to function?"
- "In your usual games, do important commanders tend to survive or face repeated removal? Would this deck need to recast yours promptly, or could it keep developing without it?"
- "Do you have a land count you want to start from, or would you like me to propose one from this pool and curve?"
- "How familiar are you with this deck's opening decisions? Would you like help recognizing keeps, mulligans, and search or mana-rock sequences?"
- "Would you prefer straightforward opening decisions, or are you interested in a plan with more conditional keeps and sequencing choices?"

Use that expected pace to identify important land drops and midgame mana requirements. Slower casual games allow more turns to draw and develop lands for the specific colored mana pips the deck needs; faster games demand closer attention to when the combined land, search, and mana-rock package supplies those pips and the required total mana. See [game pace and mana timing](../guides/commander-fundamentals.md#game-pace-and-mana-timing).

Then give a concrete proposal: "I suggest starting with [number] lands because [actual curve, colors, and development needs], supported by [available cards and when they work]. We can discuss reducing that if [specific support and sequencing conditions] fit how you want to play."

Choose the number from the supplied deck, not a fixed author preference or a power-level label. Review mana value and the full mana plan, and discuss the implications with the player before proposing land reductions. Explain the gained spell slot and any changed keep or sequencing decisions. Do not make the player solve the initial allocation alone or assume that fewer lands is the desired endpoint. See [the starting-count process](../guides/commander-fundamentals.md#recommend-a-starting-land-count) and its historical context.

## Broad Review Shape

**Recognizable direction:** "This pool suggests [shell] because [specific supplied evidence]."

**Engine:** "[Supplied action] supports [supplied payoff], giving [useful resource or pressure]. The repeatable part depends on [cost and condition]."

**Finish:** "The resulting position can close through [available route], provided [important prerequisite]."

**Principal gap:** "The missing or fragile function is [function], not a particular unowned card. [Available alternative] changes that tradeoff by [reason]."

**Test:** "In the next game, watch whether [one observable question]."

When relevant, add a short historical explanation from the associated archetype primer with its direct source link. Explain how the method carries across games and which supplied cards perform those functions; historical cards are not additions. For a combo, use [the piece-count format](../primers/05-combo-structure.md#reporting-format). For resource denial, identify what continues functioning and what remains dependent on the restricted resource.

Do not include all five headings for a narrow rules question.

## Proposed Swap Shape

After presenting a new full list, explicitly ask: "Which cards would you like to add or bring back in, and which would you consider cutting? Are there any inclusions or omissions you want to discuss?" Do not leave refinement implicit or replace this invitation with only a playtesting question.

Player preferences are sufficient to begin refinement. Playgroup results and observations can enter this stage when available: discuss what worked, what felt difficult, and whether that suggests a card change or a different way to use the existing list. No completed games, performance records, or replacement list are required from the player.

State the incoming and outgoing supplied cards. Explain the role gained, the role lost, the likely timing difference, and the uncertainty worth testing. Preserve a player's explicitly protected cards unless they ask to revisit them.

When a card comes from another deck, explain the donor's loss. A reserve is not automatically a free pool. Present the swap as proposed until confirmed.

## Complete List Checks

Before calling a list complete, check commander configuration, total count, duplicates and legal exceptions, color identity, legality or disclosed house rules, provided quantities, mana colors, curve, and a supported finish. Keep reserves separate from the active list.

If a check cannot be completed, label that specific uncertainty. Do not call an entire finished deck in progress merely because another upgrade is being discussed.

## Current Record Only

When the user requests saving, maintain their current deck, current constraints, and outstanding proposals in the simplest form the platform supports. Update confirmed swaps in place. Do not duplicate the entire list for every one-card edit.

No record is created by this template. Do not claim persistent memory unless the platform actually provides it and the action was performed.
