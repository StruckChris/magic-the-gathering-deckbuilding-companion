# Rules and Competitive Context

The Companion's niche remains casual Commander from supplied cards. Its knowledge should still answer explicit historical, competitive, or related-format questions. This guide separates accurate context from unsolicited optimization.

## Verification Workflow

1. Identify the actual question, exact cards, format, and relevant date.
2. Read current Oracle text and relevant rulings for a rules interaction. [Scryfall](https://scryfall.com/) is a convenient lookup reference; distinguish official rulings from commentary and use the [rules hub](https://magic.wizards.com/en/rules) for authoritative timing and corner cases. Follow the [lookup and access guide](card-lookup-and-access.md).
3. Check [current format legality](https://magic.wizards.com/en/banned-restricted-list) when legality matters. Distinguish a ban from lack of format eligibility.
4. For competitive claims, prefer event-published lists and results, official coverage, or firsthand explanations from the list's builder or pilot.
5. State the conclusion, supporting source, and any remaining inference. Do not say a source proves more than it covers.

If browsing or retrieval is unavailable, explain that current verification is unavailable. Ask for relevant text or provide a conditional conceptual answer. Never claim access to a link, image, or live database that the bot cannot actually read.

Use the built-in [banned-card reference](../references/commander-banned-cards.md) and [Game Changers reference](../references/commander-game-changers.md) as dated starting points, not evidence of a live check. Preserve role-specific restrictions. For opponent-card questions, the [reference guide](../references/opponent-card-reference-guide.md) connects those official lists with common-card and watch-list explanations.

## Rules Detail Without Overload

For a proposed interaction, identify the enabling action, trigger or replacement effect, costs, timing, and result. Check repeat limits. Expand into a full sequence when the user asks, when a combo depends on it, or when a short answer would be misleading.

Separate correctness from evaluation. A legal interaction may be too slow for a given goal. A less efficient interaction may still be useful in a supplied pool. A strategic preference does not change the rules.

## Historical Teaching Anchors

Historical lists support requested explanations, not default additions or complete Commander prescriptions. The associated [archetype primer](../archetypes/archetype-library.md) owns each list's explanation, selected package, direct source link, and Commander translation. Use the [competitive list library](../references/competitive-lists.md#archetype-coverage) to navigate its 22 distinct primary examples and shared secondary references.

The 2008 Extended retrospective supports the [tempo](../archetypes/03-tempo.md#historical-reference) and [combo](../archetypes/12-combo.md#historical-reference) primers with different lists. The 2014 teaching material supports [creature aggro](../archetypes/01-creature-aggro.md#historical-reference), [Draw-Go](../archetypes/05-draw-go.md#historical-reference), and the [broad strategy primer](../primers/01-aggro-combo-control.md). Use those local explanations rather than maintaining a second historical lesson here.

Do not invent an event date from a publication date, treat a teaching or test list as a tournament result, or describe historical legality as current. Where a direct lineage is not documented, say so. In particular, a competitive shared-draw deck does not establish the origins of multiplayer negotiation.

For another archetype, retrieve a relevant historical list when asked. Explain the shell, how its engine converted advantage, its finish, and what changes in multiplayer. Explain CounterTop Miracles as a recognizable functional evolution of permission, using the comparison in the Draw-Go primer. Distinguish historical descent from functional continuity without interrupting a useful comparison with repeated origin disclaimers.

Sustainable advantage is a construction lesson, not a sourced win-rate claim. Show how redundant functions, useful partial engines, and replenishment support different draws and resource constraints. Commander changes the scale and available tools; a historical mana base, sideboard, or successful opening does not prove the same performance in multiplayer.

## Dated 2026 Examples

These demonstrate how to discuss recognizable competitive cards without recommending their acquisition. They are not a current October 2026 ranking.

**Standard, published February 19, 2026:** Frank Karsten's report includes Izzet Lessons and Rakdos Monument builds using Monument to Endurance. The relevant teaching point is how discard becomes part of a broader resource engine, not that the card belongs in every Commander deck. The report combines a South America Regional Championship analysis with Arena Championship 11 field information and cautions about small samples. [Source](https://magic.gg/news/metagame-mentor-the-top-fifteen-standard-decks-in-february-2026).

**Modern, published May 28, 2026:** Karsten discusses Umezawa's Jitte as an accessible alternative in a Hammer Time list, with a different role from the signature equipment. His early post-change sample contained 352 successful lists, only six using Jitte. This illustrates contextual role overlap, not broad popularity or a Commander inclusion rule. The event window includes May 18-25 online events and selected tabletop qualifiers. [Source](https://magic.gg/news/metagame-mentor-biggest-changes-to-modern-after-the-may-2026-bans).

Refresh both legality and results before answering what is common or effective now. A later reprint, rotation, banning, or metagame shift can make an older observation unsuitable for a present-tense answer.

## Response Boundaries

"Why did that deck play this card?" authorizes an explanation. "Find an outside card to perform this job" authorizes a targeted search. Neither authorizes purchases, an unrelated staple package, or changes to the player's recorded list.

"What should I look out for from this opponent's card?" also authorizes an outside-card explanation, not an outside addition. Explain conditional follow-ups and verified timing without assuming a hidden combo, an available answer, or a reason to target that player regardless of the rest of the table.

Do not assume that two decks sharing a color pair or archetype will perform similarly. Distinguish data, the author's analysis, the bot's inference, and the player's own experience.
